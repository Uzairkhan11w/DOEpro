01The workflow

From spreadsheet
to publishable table

Four steps, in the order of the tabs. Every result comes with a short reading of what it shows, worded to match the evidence: a test that is not significant reads as insufficient evidence, never as proof of no difference.

  1. 01

    Load

    Paste straight from Excel, upload a CSV or start from a worked example. A data check finds decimal commas, units typed after numbers and labels that differ only in capitals, and corrects only what is certain.

  2. 02

    Explore and check

    Look before you test: a quick summary and a screening of each response as soon as the data are in, then graphs, correlation, regression, principal components, cluster and factor analysis, and the checks of the assumptions, each with a reading of what the numbers and plots show.

  3. 03

    Analyse

    Choose the design and map its columns. The right error terms follow: split and strip plots get their strata, and analyses over seasons or locations treat the environment as random.

  4. 04

    Report

    The ANOVA, means with SE(m), SE(d) and the comparison you choose, interaction plots and graphs to download, then an interpretation and a report to print or save as PDF.

03The engine

04The evidence

Checked against
the textbook

The worked examples of Gomez & Gomez, Statistical Procedures for Agricultural Research (1984), are reproduced exactly, with one deliberate difference: environments are treated as random, so treatments are tested against their interaction with the environment. Behind that, the package's tests check every statistic against an independent computation.

  • Completely randomised design
  • Randomised complete block design
  • Latin square
  • Split plot
  • Combined analysis over seasons
